# Retrieval evaluation (generated)

Generated by `python -m evals.retrieval_eval --report` (or `supportagent kb eval`). **Do not edit by hand**; every number below is computed by that script.
Questions and gold labels were authored by Claude (an LLM), **not by humans, and not human-reviewed**: see `evals/retrieval/PROVENANCE.md`.

## Setup

- Corpus: 53 policy sections in 9 documents (`policies/`), one chunk per `##` section, ref = `file#section`.
- Questions: 169 total = 85 dev + 84 test, stratified by category (alternating by sha256 of the id; no random seed). 26 have no answer in the corpus; 16 need more than one section.
- Dev is the only split used to choose anything (RRF k, dense weight, pool size, rerank weight, abstention thresholds). Test is evaluated once with those frozen choices.
- recall@k = share of gold sections found in the top k (multi-section questions have 2 gold refs); MRR@10 = reciprocal rank of the first gold section; nDCG@5 uses binary gains. These are computed over answerable questions only.
- No-answer precision = of the questions a system refuses to answer (top evidence below a dev-tuned threshold), the share that truly have no answer; no-answer recall = share of unanswerable questions it refuses. n/a = never abstained.
- Intervals are 95% percentile bootstrap CIs over questions (2000 resamples, fixed seed). With ~84 test questions (only ~13 unanswerable) the intervals are wide; differences smaller than the intervals are not evidence.
- Environment: Python 3.10.11, fastembed 0.9.1, onnxruntime 1.23.2, sentence-transformers None.

## Caveats

- Authored by the same model that wrote the retrieval code, with no human labelling; gold labels can be wrong or arguable, and phrasing may echo the author's own vocabulary.
- ~170 questions over 53 sections: CIs are wide and many cells are tiny. No claim beyond what the intervals support.
- The thesaurus and phrase rules were written by the same author after the question set existed (not blind); only numeric knobs were tuned on dev, but rule authoring itself is a leak the split cannot remove.
- Corpus is synthetic and small; absolute numbers say little about a real knowledge base.
